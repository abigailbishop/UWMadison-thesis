\section{Cosmic Rays and Ultra-High Energy Neutrinos}

  Cosmic rays (charged particles from outer space) have been studied since the early 1900s when scientists first discovered their radiation on Earth. 
  It was initially thought that the radiation was coming from the Earth itself, so Victor Hess launched a balloon-based experiment that surprisingly indicated the radiation did not decrease with altitude, but increased instead. 
  From this point, it was known that the observed radiation from cosmic rays was extraterrestrial. 
  Cosmic rays, especially at medium and low energies affect life on Earth, for example, in the form of aurora borealis, electronics in satellites, and the radiation-related safety of astronauts in space. 
  Scientists also use them to further their understanding of astronomical objects and events. 
  As experiments have advanced in technology and scale since Victor Hess' observations, the sources and energy flux of cosmic rays have been theorized and constrained. 
  In the flux of cosmic rays, there are two distinct features resulting from the high energy transition from cosmic rays accelerated by sources in the Milky Way (our galaxy) to extragalactic cosmic rays called the "knee" and the transition at ultra-high energies from cosmic rays with a lower proton number to cosmic rays with a greater proton number called the "ankle". 
  The highest energy cosmic ray ever detected was the Oh-My-God particle, observed in 1991 by the Fly's Eye camera and estimated to have had an energy of $3.1\times10^{20}$ eV. 
  Since then, many detectors have been created to search for cosmic rays of all energies, though this thesis focuses on the study of byproducts from ultra-high energy cosmic rays (UHECR).

  \subsection{In-Air Observations of Ice}
  
    A method for observing a greater volume of ice is to mount radio antennas to an atmospheric balloon. 
    These weather balloons are only likely to observe events that are earth-skimming due to the geometric constraints of the Askaryan Cone in ice. 
    One other downside is that the weather balloon often only flies for a couple of weeks before running out of the air needed to stay aloft. % Check this duration
    The gain in observation volume far outweighs the limited flight duration and has led to many successful flights and interesting results.
