\chapter{Conclusions and Outlook \label{chap:conclusion}}

This thesis focused on the search for ultra-high energy (UHE) neutrinos in ice via Askaryan emission.
These neutrinos originate in extreme astrophysical sources (e.g. active galactic nuclei) or cosmogenically when UHE cosmic rays (UHECRs) interact with photons of the Cosmic Microwave Background. 
I worked mainly with the Askaryan Radio Array (ARA) but also with the Radio Neutrino Observatory of Greenland (RNOG) and IceCube-Gen2 to simulate present and potential neutrino detectors; develop, assemble, and maintain detector hardware; and search for neutrinos in over one decade of experimental data.
Graduate projects\footnote{\comment{The rest of this paragraph feels random, could be rephrased.}} that we're not presented in this thesis include the GPS survey of ARA, service work managing data for ARA and rootifying PA data for analysis, and my work configuring the ARA simulation package to import externally-simulated electric fields enabling integration of cosmic ray simulations like Corsika and FAERIE with ARA detector simulations. 

ARA is comprised of 5 radio stations and has taken data since 2012. 
Updates to simulations of the array were discussed, highlighting the importance of simulating the outgoing muons and taus from charged current neutrino interactions of the same flavors and the benefits of simulating these events in an array-wide context which considers an event's ability to trigger multiple stations. 
For example, secondary particle interactions make up 40\% of triggered events at $10^{21}$~eV and 35\% of triggered events trigger on multiple ARA stations at the same energy~\citep{ara_veff}.
We also calculated that ARA is expected to have triggered on 2.27 neutrinos according to the pure-proton cosmogenic neutrino flux model using an optimistic maximum proton acceleration energy of $E_{\text{max}}=10^{21}$~eV from \citep{flux-kotera}. 
These trigger level figures demonstrate the value of the ARA dataset and the goal that neutrino searches should strive for~\citep{ara_secondaries, ara_veff}. 

This thesis centers around a neutrino search through 10.6 years of ARA data taken by all stations~\citep{ara_5sa-icrc}. 
10\% of the data were used to create datasets that resemble backgrounds while simulations were used to create neutrino signal datasets.
A 19-variable Linear Discriminant Analysis was trained to discriminate thermal background events from simulated neutrino signal events. 
The optimization of this analysis in conjunction with surface background cuts led to an expected background rate of 0.13 events and a signal efficiency of \comment{$\sim 35$\%}.
When the analysis was applied to the full dataset, one event passed all cuts on the Phased Array (PA) detector which was later realized to be a hardware glitch on the colocated A5 station, a glitch that already has a dedicated cut for stations like A5.
After expanding this cut, a posteriori, to remove PA events within 0.1~s of A5 events removed by this glitch cut, the PA event no longer passes all analysis cuts.

We proceed to calculate the Feldman-Cousins 90\% confidence level intervals based on the observation of 0 events and an expected background of 0.13 events, leading to an upper limit based an underfluctuation of 2.44 expected events\footnote{\comment{I've been working with Marco to figure out how to phrase this correctly. I doubt I have it, still.}}~\citep{feldman_cousins}. 
Our analysis sets the world-leading upper limit on the cosmic neutrino flux above $4 \times 10^{19}$~eV and was expected to observe 0.41 events at analysis level according to the \citep{flux-kotera} model discussed earlier. 
Additionally, we estimate that this analysis would have identified 1.75 neutrino candidates if subjected to the flux established from the UHE neutrino observation of KM3NeT \citep{km3net_neutrino}, 2.79 if subjected to the flux established by \citep{flux-muzio}, and 6.24 if subjected to the upper limit formed by \citep{icecube_search-ehe} and \citep{anita_iv} which can be compared to our upper limit based on 2.44 events \comment{idk about this phrasing}.
With the addition of the upper limit established by our analysis to the field, UHE radio detectors are on the precipice of probing theoretical UHE neutrino flux models at the highest energies which expands both our knowledge of cosmic neutrinos but also the cosmic rays that produce many of them.

This paragraph summarizes some reassurance, suggestions, and ideas for future analyses and detectors.
Firstly, one silver lining of the PA event that initially passed the analysis is that it was up-traveling, coherent radiation - exactly the kind of signal we search for. 
This serves as a proof of concept for the strategy developed for this analysis.

We also recommend installing detectors more than 5 kilometers from sources of expected anthropogenic backgrounds. 
Finally, the analysis involved a great number of station-level and array-wide configurations which drove up simulation and analysis optimization complexity. 
We recommend that present and future detectors minimize changes that will motivate the creation of new station configurations (e.g. updates to station hardware and data taking settings). 
The gold standard to strive for is installing a detector in an optimized state and leaving it for as long as possible as such. 
If a change is necessary, survey all stations for necessary changes and update them all at once, so the array wide configuration is only updated once.

The field of UHE neutrino astrophysics is a growing field still in the discovery phase. 
As many UHE neutrinos are expected to come from light UHECRs and as the Pierre Auger Observatory has published that UHECRs are heavier at the highest energies~\citep{pao_mass_composition}, we expect the flux of UHE neutrinos to be very low. 
While ARA sets the most stringent upper limit on UHE neutrinos with energies greater than $4\times 10^{19}$~eV, the under-construction RNOG experiment is expected to place upper limits that are \comment{X\%} more stringent and the proposed IceCube-Gen2 radio array will be \comment{X\%} stronger than the ARA limit presented here, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:future_sensitivity}.
With experiments like KM3NeT, IceCube, Auger and the radio detectors RNOG and the IceCube-Gen2 radio array, UHE neutrino astrophysics will evolve from publishing upper limits within the next two decades \comment{too optimistic?}. 
We will then establish the long sought after UHE neutrino flux and use our observations to enhance our understanding the extreme and enigmatic environments that create UHE neutrinos and UHECRs.
