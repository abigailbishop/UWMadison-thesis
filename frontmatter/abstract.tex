Ultra-high energy (UHE, energy > 100~PeV) neutrinos are a rare piece of multi-messenger astrophysics, especially valuable for understanding the UHE cosmic ray flux composition. 
When UHE neutrinos interact with matter, they initiate particle cascades that emit optical-wavelength Cherenkov and radio-wavelength Askaryan emission in dielectric media, such as ice.
Searching for UHE neutrino emission in ice is advantageous because the long attenuation and scattering lengths for radio emission in ice allow a small detector to monitor Askaryan emission across many cubic kilometers of ice, as opposed to optical-based detectors which can only monitor Cherenkov emission over a few tens of meters.
Radio-based UHE neutrino detectors have observed Askaryan emission in ice from cosmic ray showers \cite{ara_cr, arianna_cr, anita_cr} but none have observed a neutrino candidate in ice. 
The highest energy neutrino ever observed was detected in the Mediterranean Sea by KM3NeT using a UHE outgoing muon from the neutrino's inferred interaction in the Earth \cite{km3net}. 
Based on the $\sim$120~PeV outgoing muon, the original neutrino's energy is estimated to be $\sim$220~PeV. 
This thesis comprises simulations and analyses dedicated to finding UHE neutrinos using in-ice detectors at the South Pole and at Greenland. 
It begins with an introduction to field of UHE neutrino observations and it's place in multi-messenger astrophysics. 
Then a study simulating IceCube-Gen2 (a proposed in-ice optical neutrino observatory) with radio antennas accompanying all optical modules for joint Askaryan and Cherenkov emission observations. 
Next the Askaryan Radio Array (ARA) is described and a literature review of previous results is presented.
The following chapter details simulation studies of ARA's sensitivity, featuring the additional simulation of outgoing particles so that events like KM3NeT's UHE neutrino are considered in ARA sensitivity and characterized for potential observations. 
This next two chapters of this thesis cover the data cleaning and analysis of ARA's second station in the context of an array-wide, decadal neutrino search over all 5 ARA stations.
The thesis then concludes by summarizing results from this paper and presenting next steps and suggestions for future detectors and UHE neutrino analyses, especially in the context of the Radio Neutrino Observatory of Greenland and IceCube-Gen2 Radio \cite{rnog_detector, icecube_gen2_wp}.
